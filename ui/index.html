Tips for good results

Say the timeframe and the market. "1 hour chart, BTCUSDT" avoids guesses.

Say how you exit, not just how you enter. A stop-loss and a target, or a rule like "sell when RSI goes above 70".

Say how much you risk. "Risk 1% per trade" is safer and easier to reason about than a fixed size.

Be careful with leverage. At 10x, a 10% move against you wipes out the position. The summary shows the liquidation distance.

Check the summary. If it doesn't match what you meant, tell the assistant what's wrong, or fix it in the builder.

Want to wire an AI straight in? The prompt is also served at /api/prompt, and the file format is a JSON Schema.

How candlerail tests a strategy

Backtests are only useful if they're honest. These are the rules every test follows.

The simulation

Decisions happen at the close. Rules are checked when a candle closes, using only data up to that moment.

Orders fill at the next open. A signal on the 14:00 candle trades at the 15:00 open, never at a price you couldn't have had.

Stops are checked against each candle's range. If one candle touches both your stop and your target, the stop counts, because we can't know which came first. A gap through a stop fills at the worse open.

Every fill pays fees, and market and stop orders pay slippage. Defaults are 0.1% fee and 0.02% slippage, typical for retail crypto. Change them in the builder.

Leverage uses isolated margin. Each position has a liquidation price. If the market reaches it, that trade loses its whole margin, and the rest of the account is untouched.

Warm-up is respected. A 200-bar average has no value for the first 199 candles, so rules that use it stay off until then.

Reading the results

Look at max drawdown before return: it's how much pain you'd have sat through. Look at trade count: fewer than 30 trades says little. Compare with buy and hold: many strategies don't beat it. And check the two halves of the period: if it only works on the first, it was fitted to the past.

Simulation only. candlerail simulates trades and never sends orders. Past results don't predict future ones, and most leveraged traders lose money. Nothing here is financial advice.

Words you'll see

Long / shortLong profits when price rises. Short profits when it falls.
Stop-lossCloses a losing trade at a set distance, so one bad trade can't sink the account.
Take-profitCloses a winning trade at a target price.
Trailing stopA stop that follows price up (for longs) and never moves back.
Risk per tradeHow much of the account you lose if the stop is hit. 1% is a common choice.
Risk multiple (R)Target distance as a multiple of the stop distance. 2R means "risk 1 to make 2".
LeverageBorrowing to take a bigger position. 5x means a 1% move changes your margin by 5%.
LiquidationThe exchange closes a leveraged trade when losses use up its margin.
DrawdownFall from the account's highest point. Max drawdown is the worst one.
Win rateShare of trades that made money. Good strategies can have low win rates if winners are big.
Profit factorTotal won divided by total lost. Above 1 made money; above 1.5 is solid.
Sharpe ratioReturn per unit of volatility, annualised. Above 1 is decent for a single strategy.
OverfittingTuning rules until they fit past data perfectly, so they fail on new data.
Crosses aboveWas below on the previous candle, is above on this one.
