candlerail
Build and backtest trading strategies. No code needed.
Welcome

Turn a trading idea into a tested strategy in minutes.

Pick a template, change it with dropdowns, or describe it in your own words. candlerail replays years of real prices through your rules, with fees, slippage and leverage, and tells you plainly whether the idea holds up.

What you can build

  • Price action: candle patterns, swing support and resistance, market structure, previous day, week and month levels, opening ranges, volume spikes.
  • Indicators: moving averages, RSI, MACD, Bollinger, ATR, stochastic, VWAP, SuperTrend, ADX and more.
  • Any timeframe: 1-minute scalping to weekly swing trades, combining levels from higher timeframes.
  • Long and short, with leverage and a liquidation price you can see.
  • Exits: stops under a candle or a swing low, targets at resistance or at 2R, trailing stops, time limits.
  • Money management: risk per trade, daily and monthly loss limits, a drawdown circuit breaker, pauses after losing streaks.

Your data, your machine

Everything runs on this computer. Candles for any Binance pair download free and are cached; for stocks, forex or futures, upload a CSV in the builder.

Simulation only. candlerail never places real orders. Past results don't predict future ones, and most traders who use leverage lose money. Treat every result as a hypothesis to keep testing, not as advice.

Tips for good results

Say the timeframe and the market. "1 hour chart, BTCUSDT" avoids guesses.

Say how you exit, not just how you enter. A stop-loss and a target, or a rule like "sell when RSI goes above 70".

Say how much you risk. "Risk 1% per trade" is safer and easier to reason about than a fixed size.

Be careful with leverage. At 10x, a 10% move against you wipes out the position. The summary shows the liquidation distance.

Check the summary. If it doesn't match what you meant, tell the assistant what's wrong, or fix it in the builder.

Want to wire an AI straight in? The prompt is also served at /api/prompt, and the file format is a JSON Schema.

Price action

Trade what the candles say

Price action trading reads raw price, with no indicators. Where are buyers and sellers defending (support and resistance)? Which way is the market stepping (structure)? What did this candle just show (its shape)? Every rule below is plain price, so you can build any setup you'd spot on a chart.

1. Read one candle

Every candle gives these values, ready to use in rules.

Combine them with numbers: lower_wick >= 2 * body is a rejection wick, range > 2 * range[1] is a candle twice as big as the last, close > high[1] closes above the previous high.

2. Find the levels

  • Swing support and resistance (swings): the latest swing low and high. A swing needs right candles after it to be confirmed, so nothing looks into the future.
  • Market structure (swings.structure): 1 when highs and lows are both rising, −1 when both falling.
  • Higher-timeframe levels (period): the previous hour, 4 hours, day, week or month's open, high, low and close, usable on any chart. Trade 5-minute candles against yesterday's high, or daily candles against last month's.
  • Opening range (opening_range): the high and low of the first minutes of each session, for crypto or stock market opens.
  • Volume (volume_avg.relative): 2 means twice the usual volume, a sign the move has conviction.

3. Know the patterns

Add a patterns indicator (say with id pa) and use any of these as pa.hammer == 1. The last candle drawn is the one that completes the pattern.

4. Build your own setup in four questions

1

Where?

At which level does the idea make sense? A swing low, yesterday's high, the opening range, last month's high.

low <= 1.003 * sw.support
2

What happened?

What does price have to do there? A rejection candle, an engulfing, a close through the level.

pa.hammer == 1
3

Any confirmation?

What makes it more likely? Structure in your favour, a volume spike, a bigger than usual candle.

vol.relative > 1.5
4

Where am I wrong?

Put the stop where the idea is invalid (under the candle or level) and the target at the next level or 2R.

"stop_loss": { "below": "low" }

Worked example: "buy a rejection of yesterday's low"

Where: price dips below yesterday's low. What: it closes back above it with a long lower wick. Confirmation: more volume than usual. Wrong if: it trades back under the wick (the stop sits a tenth of the candle's range below its low). Target: twice the risk.

Money management

    
How much to risk decides whether you survive

    
Two traders with the same entries can end up in very different places because of position size. Professional traders fix how much they can lose on one trade, one day and one month before they think about how much they can make. Here are the rules they use, and tools to apply them.

Reward, risk and the win rate you need

      
With a 1:2 risk/reward you only need to win a third of the time to break even, before costs.

Losses compound against you: a 50% loss needs a 100% gain to get back. That's why small, fixed risk per trade matters.

Rules famous traders use

    

      
The 1–2% rule (fixed fractional)
Risk a fixed small share of the account on every trade, usually 1%, and never more than 2%. Size = account × risk% ÷ distance to stop. Ten losses in a row then cost about 10%, not the account.
In candlerail: sizing risk_percent with a stop-loss.

      
Elder's 2% and 6% rules
Alexander Elder's pair of limits: no single trade may risk more than 2% of equity, and once losses in a month reach 6%, stop trading until next month. The second rule stops a bad month from turning into a disaster.
In candlerail: risk_percent ≤ 2 and risk.monthly_loss_percent: 6.

      
The Turtle traders' units
Richard Dennis's Turtles sized positions by volatility: one "unit" risked 1% of equity per 1 N, where N is the 20-day ATR, with stops 2N away. Volatile markets got smaller positions, so every market carried the same risk.
In candlerail: risk_percent with an ATR stop ("atr": 2). See the Donchian template.

      
R-multiples (Van Tharp)
Measure every trade in units of the risk taken: 1R is what you lose at the stop. A +3R trade made three times the risk. Expectancy is the average R per trade, and it lets you compare strategies whatever their size.
In candlerail: every trade shows its R, and results show the average R.

      
Kelly criterion (and half-Kelly)
A formula for the fraction of capital to risk that maximises long-run growth: f = W − (1 − W) ÷ R, with W the win rate and R the average win ÷ average loss. Full Kelly swings violently and assumes your statistics are exact, so traders use half or less.
In candlerail: results show the Kelly estimate and warn if you risk more than half of it.

      
Fixed ratio (Ryan Jones)
For futures traders who trade whole contracts: add a contract each time profits grow by a set amount ("delta") times the number of contracts already traded. Size grows more slowly than with a percentage, and keeps early drawdowns small.
Use the position calculator above with futures to see contract counts.

      
Martingale vs anti-martingale
Martingale doubles the size after each loss to win it back. One long losing streak, which always comes eventually, wipes out the account. Anti-martingale does the opposite: size stays small after losses and grows with profits, as trend followers do when they add to winners.
candlerail has no martingale sizing, deliberately. risk.pause_after_losses steps back after a streak instead.

      
Daily limits and circuit breakers
Many proprietary trading firms hold traders to a daily loss limit (often 2 to 5%) and a maximum drawdown (often 5 to 10%). Hit it and you stop for the day, or for good. It removes the worst decisions, which usually come right after big losses.
In candlerail: risk.daily_loss_percent, risk.max_drawdown_percent, risk.max_trades_per_day.

    

  


  

    

      
Different markets need different sizes

      
The more a market moves in a day, the smaller the position for the same risk. Typical figures, to calibrate expectations:

      

        
MarketDaily moveCommon risk / tradeLeverage to watch

        
          
Large-cap stocks, ETFs1–2%0.5–1%US margin: 2x overnight, up to 4x intraday for pattern day traders

          
Forex majors0.5–1%0.5–1%Retail caps: 30:1 (EU, UK), 50:1 (US). Sized in lots: 1 standard lot = 100,000 units, ≈ $10 per pip on EURUSD

          
Crypto3–5% or more0.25–1%Exchanges offer 50–100x; liquidation comes fast. Most careful traders stay at 1–3x

          
Index futures1–2%0.5–1%Priced per point (E-mini S&P 500: $50 a point), so one contract can be a large position

          
Commodities1–3%, with gaps0.5–1%Overnight gaps can jump past stops; size for the gap, not just the stop

        
      

    

    

      
Spreading risk across markets and sectors

      
    
        
  • Correlated positions count as one. Long three tech stocks, or BTC, ETH and SOL together, is close to one big bet. Add up their risk.
    • 
        
  • Cap total open risk ("portfolio heat"). A common ceiling is 5–6% of the account at risk across all open trades at once.
    • 
        
  • Cap each sector. Many portfolio managers keep any one sector to roughly 20–25% of exposure, so one bad industry can't sink everything.
    • 
        
  • Size down in volatile regimes. When the market's daily range doubles, halve the position to keep the same risk (the Turtle approach).
    • 
        
  • Leveraged markets deserve less, not more. Forex and crypto allow huge leverage, which is why traders there usually risk less per trade.
    • 
      

      
These are widely used guidelines for thinking about risk, not personal financial advice.

How candlerail tests a strategy
Backtests are only useful if they're honest. These are the rules every test follows.

  

    

      
The simulation

      
Decisions happen at the close. Rules are checked when a candle closes, using only data up to that moment.

      
Orders fill at the next open. A signal on the 14:00 candle trades at the 15:00 open, never at a price you couldn't have had.

      
Stops are checked against each candle's range. If one candle touches both your stop and your target, the stop counts, because we can't know which came first. A gap through a stop fills at the worse open.

      
Every fill pays fees, and market and stop orders pay slippage. Defaults are 0.1% fee and 0.02% slippage, typical for retail crypto. Change them in the builder.

      
Leverage uses isolated margin. Each position has a liquidation price. If the market reaches it, that trade loses its whole margin, and the rest of the account is untouched.

      
Warm-up is respected. A 200-bar average has no value for the first 199 candles, so rules that use it stay off until then.

      
Reading the results

      
Look at max drawdown before return: it's how much pain you'd have sat through. Look at trade count: fewer than 30 trades says little. Compare with buy and hold: many strategies don't beat it. And check the two halves of the period: if it only works on the first, it was fitted to the past.

      
Simulation only. candlerail simulates trades and never sends orders. Past results don't predict future ones, and most leveraged traders lose money. Nothing here is financial advice.

    

    

      
Words you'll see

      

        
Long / shortLong profits when price rises. Short profits when it falls.

        
Stop-lossCloses a losing trade at a set distance, so one bad trade can't sink the account.

        
Take-profitCloses a winning trade at a target price.

        
Trailing stopA stop that follows price up (for longs) and never moves back.

        
Risk per tradeHow much of the account you lose if the stop is hit. 1% is a common choice.

        
Risk multiple (R)Target distance as a multiple of the stop distance. 2R means "risk 1 to make 2".

        
LeverageBorrowing to take a bigger position. 5x means a 1% move changes your margin by 5%.

        
LiquidationThe exchange closes a leveraged trade when losses use up its margin.

        
DrawdownFall from the account's highest point. Max drawdown is the worst one.

        
Win rateShare of trades that made money. Good strategies can have low win rates if winners are big.

        
Profit factorTotal won divided by total lost. Above 1 made money; above 1.5 is solid.

        
Sharpe ratioReturn per unit of volatility, annualised. Above 1 is decent for a single strategy.

        
OverfittingTuning rules until they fit past data perfectly, so they fail on new data.

        
Crosses aboveWas below on the previous candle, is above on this one.
