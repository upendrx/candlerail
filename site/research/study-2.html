In plain words
  • We tested 11 popular swing and trend strategies, which hold trades for days or weeks, on 10 coins.
  • Trend following on 4-hour candles made money in 2020 to 2023, 2023 to 2025 and a falling 2025 to 2026, including years it was never chosen on.
  • The spot rule (Keltner breakout, long only) averaged +1.2% a month: about $6 a month on $500, with a worst month of about −$20.
  • It loses often in small amounts and earns most of its money in a few strong trends.

1The case

Study 1 showed that intraday setups lose to fees. The natural next step is fewer, longer trades, where a round trip costs a small share of what each trade risks.

Swing and trend strategies hold for days or weeks. They are old, public and simple, which is a reason for doubt: if they worked, would the edge not be gone? This study tests them the same way as Study 1, on a period long enough to include a rising market, a falling one and a crash.

Hypothesis

Some well-known trend or mean-reversion rules average at least +0.05R a trade on most of ten coins, keep a profit in a held-out year, and keep it again on years they never saw.

2What was tested

Eleven strategies in three kinds. The drawings show the long side of six of them.

Trend

SuperTrend flip

A trailing line an ATR multiple from price. Long when price closes above it, short when below.

Trend

Donchian breakout

Buy a close above the highest high of 20 (or 55) candles; exit at the lowest low of 10 (or 20).

Trend

Keltner channel breakout

Buy a close above the 20 EMA plus 2 ATR; exit below the middle line. The spot pick.

Trend

Moving average crossover

Long while the fast EMA is above the slow one: 10/30, 20/50 or 50/200.

Mean reversion

RSI(2) pullback

Above the 200-candle average, buy when the 2-period RSI falls under 10 (or 5); sell on a bounce.

Mean reversion

Bollinger reversion

In quiet markets (ADX under 20), buy under the lower band and sell at the middle.

Also tested: price crossing its 50, 100 or 200-candle average, the weekly high breakout, a MACD cross with the 200 EMA, an RSI(14) pullback in a trend, and Study 1's pin bar at a swing level with a 2R target.

Every strategy crossed with

Side

Long only (a spot account) or long and short (futures at 1×, no leverage).

Stop

2 or 3 ATR from the entry, with or without a 3 ATR trailing stop.

Markets

BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP, BNB, DOGE, ADA, AVAX, LINK and LTC on 1h, 4h and 1d candles.

Risk

Each coin trades a tenth of the account and risks 2% of that tenth per trade.

3How it was tested

Costs are the same as in Study 1: spot taker 0.1% plus 0.02% slippage, futures taker 0.05% plus 0.01%. Funding payments on futures are not included; section 7 measures them on their own.

In account terms

On a $500 account the futures pick's average month is about $12, and its worst month about −$45. Returns and losses scale roughly with the risk per trade.

7Funding-rate carry

A different kind of return: buy spot and short the same amount of the perpetual future, half the account in each leg. Price moves cancel; the short collects the funding that long traders pay every eight hours. Fees and slippage are charged on both legs.

Steady but small: about +3% a year recently and +6% to +7% in 2020 to 2023, when leveraged longs paid more to borrow. Holding only while the 7-day funding rate was positive did not help recently. Funding history comes from Binance's public futures API.

8Conclusions

  • Trend following on 4-hour candles is the one family that held up across a bull market, a crash and a falling year, on data it wasn't chosen on.
  • The edge is modest and lumpy: about +2.5% a month on average, with long flat stretches and months of −9%.
  • Long and short beat long only in the falling year; on spot, the Keltner breakout lost the least.
  • Carry is steady but small, a few percent a year: a place to park money, not a way to grow it.

Limitations

The ten coins are ones that still trade today; coins that collapsed (LUNA, FTT) aren't included, which flatters every strategy, buying and holding included. Study 3 and Study 4 add dead coins. Futures funding costs are not included in the directional backtests.
