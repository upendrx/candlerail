Study 3 · Portfolios and signals

How quants scale up, and what one account can use

Where do the large algorithmic profits in crypto come from, which other signals and instruments help, and what can a single account running an automated strategy on Binance realistically achieve?

In plain words
  • Big trading firms profit from speed and size a small account can't match. What a small account can copy is combining several slow, different strategies.
  • Holding the 5 strongest of 43 coins each week, only while bitcoin is rising, made money but with large swings.
  • Combined with the trend rule from Study 2, the account averaged +4.4% a month with a largest fall of 20%, against 73% for just holding bitcoin.
  • Market sentiment, time of day and pairs trading didn't help after fees.

1The case

Headlines about trading firms making millions a day are true, but most of those profits come from businesses a single account can't copy.

Not copyable

Market making

Quoting both sides all day for fee rebates. Needs the top fee tiers and co-located servers.

Not copyable

Speed arbitrage

Price gaps between exchanges that last milliseconds.

Partly

Funding and basis

Collecting what leveraged traders pay. Copyable, but the return is small at retail fees (Study 2).

Copyable

Systematic portfolios

Several independent, slow edges sized together. Tested here.

Hypothesis

Signals beyond one coin's own chart (the relative strength of coins, market sentiment, the clock, the spread between related coins) carry edges that survive costs. Combined with the Study 2 trend rule, they give a smoother account than any one rule alone.

2How it was tested

These are portfolio tests: each variant holds positions across coins at once and is scored on the account's monthly returns, with fees and slippage on every change. The 43-coin universe includes coins that collapsed or were delisted (FTT, LUNA, SRM, MATIC, FTM, EOS), so holding a dying coin counts.

3Coin rotation

Every 1, 7 or 14 days, rank the coins by their return over the last 7 to 90 days and hold the strongest 3, 5 or 10, optionally only while BTC is above its 50- or 100-day average. Variants also tried buying the weakest (reversal) and shorting the bottom of the list (futures).

4Fear & Greed sentiment

The free daily Crypto Fear & Greed index (alternative.me) timing BTC and ETH: buy extreme fear, buy only while below a greed cap, buy fear in an uptrend, or short greed in a downtrend. 186 variants.

5Time of day and day of week

Learn the best hours or weekdays for BTC and ETH before the split and hold only then, optionally shorting the worst ones. Run twice: with futures costs, and with no costs at all to see whether any pattern exists.

A pattern that reversed

With no costs, the best hour-based variants had a Sharpe ratio above 2.4 before the split and lost money after it (Sharpe −0.2 to −0.4). With costs, no hour-based variant made money even before the split. The weekday variants that survived are mostly just holding the market four or five days a week.

6Pairs trading

For pairs whose daily returns were correlated above 0.75 before the split (BTC/ETH, BTC/LTC, ETH/LTC, XRP/XLM, LTC/BCH), short the one that ran ahead and buy the laggard when their price ratio is 1.5 to 2.5 standard deviations from normal.

9Conclusions

  • Relative strength across many coins is a real, if volatile, edge, and it worked best with a BTC trend filter and long only.
  • Sentiment and the clock added little. What looked like an edge was the trend filter, or noise that reversed.
  • Pairs trading didn't survive retail costs on these pairs.
  • Combining two loosely related edges is the largest improvement in this study: the combined plan kept most of the return with a largest fall of about 20%, against 73% for holding BTC.

Limitations

The rotation pick was chosen among 540 variants, and the combination was built from two picks, so both carry some selection bias even after the split. Rotation trades smaller coins, where slippage at larger size would be higher than modelled.
