Study 1 · Day trading

Intraday reversals on major coins

Can well-known day-trading setups, mostly candle reversals at support and resistance, make a daily profit on BTC, ETH, SOL and XRP once real trading costs are paid?

26,040 backtests15 setups, long and short1m, 5m, 15m, 30m, 1h90 days to 27 Sep 2026
Verdict: no

No setup gave a repeatable daily profit.

One variant out of 3,255 passed selection and a held-out month. It then lost money on five other coins and on the three months before.

In plain words
  • We tested the classic day-trading patterns (hammers, engulfing candles and others at support and resistance) on Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana and XRP, on 1-minute to 1-hour charts.
  • After Binance's fees, none of them made money reliably. On short charts the fee on each trade is almost as large as what the trade risks.
  • The one rule that passed its test then lost money on other coins and earlier months: it was luck.
  • For a small account: day trading these patterns with market orders is likely to lose money slowly through fees.

1The case

Short-timeframe reversal setups are the most widely taught form of trading: find a support or resistance level, wait for a candle that shows rejection, and trade the bounce with a tight stop.

If these setups carry a real edge, it should show on the most liquid coins, at the timeframes where they are taught, after paying the fees an exchange actually charges. This study sets out to measure that edge, with a stop before a target and a daily loss limit, exactly as a disciplined day trader would run it.

Hypothesis

At least one intraday setup averages more than +0.05R a trade after costs, on most of the four coins, in both the period it was chosen on and the month after.

2What was tested

Fifteen setups, each traded long and short, plus an opening-range breakout as a trend-following reference. The drawings show the long side; the short side mirrors it.

Where the candle must appear

No setup trades a candle on its own. Each one also needs a place: the latest swing low or high (within 0.3 to 0.5 ATR of it), the previous hour's, 4 hours' or day's high or low, two ATRs from the day's VWAP, or an RSI extreme; and, in half the variants, the direction of the 8-hour trend. How these places are found, with drawings →

The other setups: morning star at a swing level, outside-bar reversal after three closes, Bollinger band pierce and reclaim, engulfing out of an RSI extreme (RSI below 30), pullback to the 20 EMA in a trend, big-small-big candles, change of character (a close above the last swing high in a falling structure) and the opening-range breakout.

Every setup crossed with

Stop

Beyond the signal candle, 1 or 1.5 ATR, 0.3% or 0.6% (round 1); 0.8%, 1.2%, 1.5 or 2 ATR (round 2).

Target

1, 1.5 or 2 times the risk. Trades close after 4 hours (round 1) or 8 hours (round 2).

Trend filter

None, or only in the direction of the 8-hour EMA.

Risk

1% of the account per trade, a 3% daily loss limit, and a one-hour pause after four losses in a row.

3How it was tested

  • Fills: signal at a candle's close, market order at the next open; the stop fills first when a candle touches both.
  • Costs: futures taker 0.05% fee and 0.01% slippage per fill; spot taker 0.1% and 0.02%. Funding is not included.
  • Trend tag: each trade is tagged by the coin's change over the 24 hours before it: above +1.5% is an uptrend, below −1.5% a downtrend, else sideways.

4Round 1: 1, 5 and 15-minute candles

465 variants × 4 coins × 3 timeframes × 2 cost levels: 11,160 backtests. Of the 1,395 variant-timeframe combinations under futures costs, 7 averaged above zero in the selection period, and none was selected.

5Round 2: wider stops

Informed only by the cost result: stops of 0.8%, 1.2%, 1.5 ATR, 2 ATR or beyond the signal candle, 5 to 60-minute candles, holds of up to 8 hours, and at least 1.5 trades a day across the four coins together. 14,880 backtests.

6The survivor on real candles

The one variant that passed both stages: pin bar at a swing level, 15-minute candles, 1.2% stop, 2R target, only with the 8-hour trend. +0.24R a trade in the selection period, +0.11R in the held-out month, profitable there on 3 of 4 coins, about 1.9 trades a day across the four.

The survivor was luck

With 1,860 combinations judged on the same month, one passing by chance is expected. It failed both independent tests.

8Conclusions

  • Don't scalp at taker fees. No candle pattern here had an edge close to what 1- and 5-minute stops pay in fees.
  • Candle reversals at support and resistance showed no reliable edge on these coins in these months, even with wide stops and before costs.
  • A good backtest isn't evidence until it repeats on data it wasn't chosen on. A study that tests thousands of variants will always find some that look good.
  • What follows: fewer, longer trades where costs are a small share of the risk. That is where Study 2 goes.

Limitations

Ninety days is a short window, and one market regime. Limit orders at the maker fee, which would lower costs, were not tested. The four coins are the largest and most efficient; smaller coins may behave differently.
